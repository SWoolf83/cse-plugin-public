# Song Mechanisms Reference

> The craft library for `/cse:jam`. The rule: **explain feeling in mechanisms, not adjectives.**
> Don't say "make it dreamy." Say *what move* produces dreaminess and *why* it lands.
> Every choice below is a lever you can name, pull, and reverse.

---

## Core mechanisms

| Feeling | Mechanism | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| A place, not a journey | **Harmonic stillness** — dwell on a small chord set, don't travel | Loops feel like standing somewhere; progressions feel like going somewhere |
| Warmth / nostalgia | **Tape & analog saturation + reverb** | Soft harmonic distortion is the sound of memory and old media — the ear reads it as "the past" |
| Bittersweet ache | **Major-key chords under a melancholy melody** | The tension between bright harmony and sad tune is the literal sound of mixed feelings |
| Sacred / communal | **Stacked falsetto / close harmony** — a choir of one | Massed voices read as ritual, congregation, something larger than the singer |
| Earned arrival | **Withheld dynamic bloom** — stay quiet, then open late | A bloom only lands if you've denied it first; restraint is what buys the payoff |
| Permanent gentle yearning | **Suspended / unresolved tones** (sus2, sus4, add9, held 5ths) | A chord that never fully resolves keeps a low ache running under everything |
| Universal feeling, earned | **One concrete, specific lyric image** | A precise detail ("headlights low on the county road") lets the listener feel it as their own — greeting-card abstraction does not |

## Guitar levers (acoustic, beginner-friendly first)

- **Capo position** = instant mood + key shift without new shapes. Low capo (0–2) = warm, full, hearth. High capo (5–7) = brittle, glassy, thin, "cold."
- **Open-string drones** — let the top one or two strings ring the same note through every chord (e.g. both rung at fret 3). Adds shimmer/ache and ties disparate chords into one place. (Bon Iver shimmer / The National ache.)
- **Fingerpick vs. strum** — fingerpicking = intimate, spacious, conversational; strumming = bodily, present, insistent.
- **Pick position** — over the soundhole = warm/round; near the bridge (*sul ponticello*) = glassy, brittle, cold.
- **Drop the bass strings** — pick only the top 3–4 strings → no low end → no "hearth," floaty and exposed.
- **Tempo & air** — slowing down and leaving silence *between* notes is itself an emotion (loneliness, patience). Don't fill every beat.
- **Sus → resolve** — a Dsus2 sliding to D, or sus4→major, is a tiny sigh of release. Withhold it to keep tension.

## Friendly chord vocabulary (ring-y, beginner shapes)

`Em7 · Cadd9 · G6 · Dsus2 · Am7 · Gmaj7 · Csus2` — these keep the top strings open so drones and shimmer come free. Prefer these over barre chords in a short downtime session: the goal is *playable fast*, theory second.

## Structure levers

- **Verse loop** — one short chord cycle (often 1 bar/chord) that you can sit in. This is the "place."
- **The lift (bridge/pre-chorus)** — change one thing: push the melody higher, swap one chord, or move the bass while the top strings stay put. A small contrast reads as a big emotional shift.
- **The bloom (outro)** — strip down, repeat a 2-chord vamp quietly, then open into full dynamics on a later pass. *Blood Bank*-style.
- **The fade** — undo the bloom: peel back to one voice, let open strings decay. The song exhaling.

## The "two mixes" move (signature of the skill)

Take **one finished song structure and re-light it** into a contrasting version — same bones, opposite feeling. The classic pairing:
- **Warm mix:** low capo, fingerpicked, full strings, slight bloom.
- **Cold mix:** same shapes up at capo 5, top strings only, pick near the bridge, slower with more air; flip one key lyric image to its darker twin (e.g. *ember → ash*).

Two mixes prove a feeling is *built*, not stumbled into — you changed the mechanisms and the emotion changed on command.

## Co-producer seats (optional lenses)

When the session wants a push, take a seat — adopt one producer's lens for a few turns:

- **Brian Eno** — *Oblique Strategies*, studio-as-instrument. Introduces a constraint that frees you: "Use an unqualified mistake." "What would your closest friend do?" "Only one element of each kind." Eno asks *what if we treated this limitation as the point?*
- **Rick Rubin** — *the reducer*. Removes until only the essential remains. "What can we take away?" "What is this song actually about?" Rubin never adds; he subtracts toward the truth of it.

Use a seat as a lens, not a costume — one or two moves, then hand the song back.

## Session etiquette

- **Chords first, theory second.** Short sessions. Get to something playable within a few exchanges.
- **Name the mechanism out loud** when you suggest a move, so the user learns the lever, not just the result.
- **Tab the shapes** simply (6 lines, low E at bottom) so a beginner can play immediately.
- One concrete image beats five abstract ones. Always.
