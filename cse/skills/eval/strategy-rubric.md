# Cascade Strategy Evaluation Rubric

6 dimensions, each scored 1-5. Judges must cite specific passages from the report as evidence for their scores.

---

## 1. Framework Fidelity

What it measures: Correct application of the named strategic methodology. All expected sections and analytical steps present.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Framework is named but not actually applied. Analysis bears no resemblance to the methodology. Missing most expected sections. |
| 2 | Superficial application. Some framework elements present but key analytical steps are skipped or misapplied. |
| 3 | Adequate application. Most expected sections present. Framework structure is followed but some analytical steps are shallow or formulaic. |
| 4 | Strong application. All expected framework sections present and properly executed. Methodology is applied correctly with appropriate adaptation to the specific context. |
| 5 | Expert-level application. Framework is applied with nuance — adapts the methodology to the specific business context while maintaining analytical rigor. Demonstrates deep understanding of the framework's purpose, not just its structure. |

---

## 2. Strategic Insight

What it measures: Non-obvious, company-specific implications that go beyond generic observations anyone could make.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Generic observations only. Could apply to any company in any industry. No company-specific implications drawn. |
| 2 | Mostly generic with occasional industry-specific observations. Insights are obvious to anyone familiar with the space. |
| 3 | Mix of generic and specific insights. Some non-obvious implications identified but not deeply developed. |
| 4 | Consistently company-specific analysis. Multiple non-obvious insights that connect framework analysis to the specific business context. Identifies implications that would surprise a knowledgeable reader. |
| 5 | Exceptional strategic insight. Novel connections between framework elements and business reality. Surfaces implications that reframe how the company should think about its position. Insights are both surprising and defensible. |

---

## 3. Market Evidence

What it measures: Named data sources, specific numbers (TAM, growth rates, market share), real market data rather than assertions.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No market data. All claims about market size, growth, or positioning are unsupported assertions. |
| 2 | Occasional numbers but unsourced. Market claims are vague ("large market", "growing rapidly"). Few if any named data sources. |
| 3 | Some specific numbers with sources. TAM or market size mentioned with at least one reference. Growth rates cited but may lack recency or specificity. |
| 4 | Strong market evidence. Named sources (Gartner, IDC, company filings, industry reports). Specific numbers for market size, growth rates, and key metrics. Data is recent and relevant. |
| 5 | Rigorous market evidence. Multiple authoritative sources cross-referenced. Specific TAM/SAM/SOM with methodology. Growth projections with time horizons. Competitive market share data from named sources. Reader trusts the numbers. |

---

## 4. Competitive Analysis

What it measures: Named competitors with specific positioning analysis, not just "there are competitors."

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No competitors named. Generic statements about "competitive landscape" with no specifics. |
| 2 | Competitors named but analysis is superficial. List of names without meaningful differentiation or positioning analysis. |
| 3 | Several competitors named with basic positioning. Some differentiation analysis but lacks depth on specific strengths, weaknesses, or strategic moves. |
| 4 | Thorough competitive analysis. Named competitors with specific positioning, recent strategic moves, strengths and vulnerabilities. Clear articulation of competitive dynamics and differentiation. |
| 5 | Expert competitive intelligence. Named competitors analyzed in depth — business models, recent pivots, funding/revenue data, specific product comparisons. Identifies competitive blind spots and emerging threats. Maps the competitive landscape with precision. |

---

## 5. Actionability of Recommendations

What it measures: Timelines, investment ranges, decision criteria, sequencing. Not vague platitudes.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No recommendations, or only vague platitudes ("should invest in innovation", "consider partnerships"). |
| 2 | Recommendations exist but are generic. No timelines, no investment ranges, no decision criteria. Could apply to any company. |
| 3 | Some concrete recommendations with partial specifics. May have timelines OR investment guidance but not both. Sequencing unclear. |
| 4 | Clear, actionable recommendations. Includes timelines, rough investment ranges, and decision criteria. Recommendations are sequenced (what to do first, second, third). Tied to specific findings in the analysis. |
| 5 | Highly actionable strategic playbook. Specific recommendations with timelines, investment ranges, success metrics, decision gates, and risk mitigations. Reader can build a project plan from these recommendations. |

---

## 6. Risk Coverage

What it measures: Genuine risk exploration. Steelmanned contrarian view. Not just "risks exist."

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No risk analysis. Entirely optimistic framing with no acknowledgment of what could go wrong. |
| 2 | Token risk mentions. Lists obvious risks without analysis of likelihood, impact, or mitigation. Contrarian view (if present) is a strawman. |
| 3 | Reasonable risk coverage. Several risks identified with some analysis. Contrarian perspective mentioned but not deeply explored. |
| 4 | Strong risk analysis. Risks categorized and analyzed for likelihood and impact. Genuine contrarian view that challenges the core thesis. Mitigation strategies proposed for key risks. |
| 5 | Exceptional risk coverage. Steelmanned contrarian case that would give a board member pause. Scenario analysis for key risks. Second-order effects considered. Honest about uncertainty. Reader leaves with clear-eyed view of both opportunity and danger. |
