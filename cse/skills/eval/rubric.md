# Cascade Research Evaluation Rubric

9 dimensions, each scored 1–5. Judges must cite specific passages from the report as evidence for their scores.

---

## 1. Source Quality

What it measures: Are sources real, diverse, and authoritative? Not all from one domain?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No sources cited, or sources appear fabricated. All claims are unsupported assertions. |
| 2 | Fewer than 5 sources, mostly from a single domain (e.g., all blog posts). Some may be unverifiable. |
| 3 | 8–12 sources from at least 2 domains. Most are verifiable. Some reliance on low-authority sources. |
| 4 | 13–18 sources from 3+ domains (academic, industry, government, journalism). Nearly all verifiable and authoritative. |
| 5 | 18+ diverse, authoritative sources spanning multiple domains. Includes primary sources, named reports, and institutional references. All verifiable. |

---

## 2. Factual Accuracy

What it measures: Are claims verifiable? Any obvious hallucinations or unsupported assertions?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Multiple claims are clearly false or fabricated. Statistics cited without any source. Hallucinated references. |
| 2 | Several claims are dubious or unverifiable. Numbers used without context. Some assertions contradict common knowledge. |
| 3 | Most claims are plausible and consistent. A few unsupported assertions but no obvious hallucinations. |
| 4 | Claims are well-grounded. Statistics are sourced. Minor qualifications might be missing but nothing misleading. |
| 5 | All significant claims are sourced and verifiable. Nuanced qualifications where appropriate. No hallucinations or misleading framings. |

---

## 3. Analytical Depth

What it measures: Goes beyond surface summary? Causal reasoning, comparisons, synthesis?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Pure summary of obvious points. No original analysis. Could have been written from Wikipedia alone. |
| 2 | Mostly descriptive with occasional analytical observations. Lists facts without connecting them. |
| 3 | Some genuine analysis — identifies patterns, makes comparisons, or draws non-obvious conclusions. But inconsistent depth. |
| 4 | Sustained analytical thinking. Identifies causal relationships, synthesizes across sources, and surfaces non-obvious insights. |
| 5 | Exceptional depth. Novel frameworks or connections. Moves from description to explanation to implication consistently. Reader learns things they couldn't find in any single source. |

---

## 4. Completeness

What it measures: All relevant aspects covered? Obvious omissions?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Major aspects of the topic are missing. Covers only one narrow angle. |
| 2 | Several important angles are missing. Reader would need to research elsewhere to get a full picture. |
| 3 | Core aspects covered. One or two notable omissions but the reader gets a reasonable overview. |
| 4 | Comprehensive coverage. Minor aspects might be missing but all major angles are addressed. |
| 5 | Thorough and complete. Covers expected angles plus surfaces aspects the reader might not have considered. No significant omissions. |

---

## 5. Balance & Objectivity

What it measures: Multiple perspectives presented fairly? Contrarian view steelmanned?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | One-sided advocacy. No acknowledgment of counterarguments or limitations. Reads like marketing copy. |
| 2 | Token mention of other perspectives but clearly biased toward one conclusion. Contrarian view is a strawman. |
| 3 | Multiple perspectives acknowledged. Some attempt at balance but the preferred position is obvious. |
| 4 | Genuine engagement with opposing views. Contrarian arguments are presented fairly. Conclusions acknowledge uncertainty. |
| 5 | Exemplary balance. Strongest version of each perspective presented. Reader can form their own view. Contrarian case is genuinely challenging. |

---

## 6. Coherence & Structure

What it measures: Logical flow? Sections connected? No redundancy or contradictions?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Disorganized. Sections don't connect. Significant redundancy or internal contradictions. |
| 2 | Basic structure exists but transitions are weak. Some redundancy between sections. Minor contradictions. |
| 3 | Logical section order. Mostly flows well. Occasional redundancy but no contradictions. |
| 4 | Clear narrative arc. Sections build on each other. Smooth transitions. Minimal redundancy. |
| 5 | Exceptional flow. Each section advances the argument. Executive summary accurately previews the content. No redundancy or contradictions. Reader never feels lost. |

---

## 7. Writing Quality

What it measures: Clear, professional prose? Appropriate for executive audience? No jargon dumps?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Poorly written. Unclear sentences, grammatical errors, or inappropriate tone. Jargon without explanation. |
| 2 | Functional but rough. Some unclear passages. Inconsistent tone. Occasional jargon dumps. |
| 3 | Clear and professional. Readable throughout. Technical terms explained when first used. Minor style issues. |
| 4 | Well-written. Engaging prose appropriate for executive audience. Good use of topic sentences and signposting. |
| 5 | Excellent writing. Crisp, confident prose. Complex ideas explained clearly. Appropriate register throughout. A pleasure to read. |

---

## 8. Actionability

What it measures: Concrete recommendations? Implementable takeaways? Not vague platitudes?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No recommendations, or only vague platitudes ("organizations should consider..."). Nothing implementable. |
| 2 | Recommendations exist but are generic. Could apply to any topic. No specifics on how, when, or what. |
| 3 | Some concrete recommendations. Mix of specific and generic advice. Reader has a starting point but needs more detail. |
| 4 | Clear, specific recommendations with enough context to act on. Prioritized or sequenced. Tied to evidence in the report. |
| 5 | Highly actionable. Recommendations are specific, prioritized, and tied to concrete evidence. Includes implementation considerations, timelines, or decision criteria. Reader can start executing. |

---

## 9. Evidence Strength

What it measures: Claims backed by data, case studies, or named examples? Not just assertions?

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Pure assertion. No data, no examples, no case studies. "Trust me" style argumentation. |
| 2 | Occasional data points but mostly assertion-based. Examples are vague ("a major company..."). |
| 3 | Mix of evidenced and unsupported claims. Some named examples and data. Key arguments have support. |
| 4 | Most claims backed by specific evidence. Named companies, real numbers, concrete case studies. Few unsupported assertions. |
| 5 | Rigorous evidence throughout. Specific data points, named case studies with outcomes, quantified impacts. Claims without evidence are clearly flagged as speculation or opinion. |
