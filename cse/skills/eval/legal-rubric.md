# Cascade Legal Analysis Evaluation Rubric

6 dimensions, each scored 1-5. Judges must cite specific passages from the report as evidence for their scores.

---

## 1. Legal Precision

What it measures: Correct application of legal concepts, statutes, regulations, and terminology. No misstatement of law.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Legal concepts are misapplied or fabricated. Statutes cited incorrectly or do not exist. Terminology used imprecisely or incorrectly. |
| 2 | Some correct legal concepts but significant errors. Statutes mentioned vaguely without proper citation. Legal terminology used loosely. |
| 3 | Generally correct legal analysis. Most citations are accurate. Terminology is properly used but analysis lacks nuance on edge cases or evolving standards. |
| 4 | Strong legal precision. Statutes and regulations cited correctly with specific section references. Legal concepts applied accurately with awareness of jurisdictional variations. |
| 5 | Expert-level precision. Correct and current application of legal standards with proper citations (statute, section, subsection). Distinguishes between settled law and evolving standards. Identifies where legal interpretation is contested. |

---

## 2. Jurisdictional Coverage

What it measures: Accounts for relevant jurisdictions, identifies conflicts of law, and flags where requirements differ.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Single jurisdiction assumed without acknowledgment. No mention of jurisdictional variations or conflicts. |
| 2 | Primary jurisdiction identified but analysis ignores other applicable jurisdictions. No conflict-of-law analysis. |
| 3 | Multiple jurisdictions acknowledged. Some key jurisdictional differences noted but coverage is incomplete or superficial. |
| 4 | Thorough multi-jurisdictional analysis. Key differences across applicable jurisdictions identified with specific examples. Conflict-of-law issues flagged where they arise. |
| 5 | Comprehensive jurisdictional coverage. All material jurisdictions analyzed with specific regulatory differences mapped. Conflict-of-law analysis with recommended approaches. Identifies emerging jurisdictional trends that could affect the analysis. |

---

## 3. Risk Identification

What it measures: Comprehensive identification of legal risks with assessment of probability, severity, and interconnections.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No meaningful risk identification. Legal risks are either ignored or listed without any assessment of likelihood or impact. |
| 2 | Obvious risks mentioned but without probability or severity analysis. Risk identification is superficial — misses non-obvious risks. |
| 3 | Reasonable risk identification. Major risks listed with some probability/severity assessment. May miss secondary or emerging risks. |
| 4 | Strong risk identification. Risks categorized and assessed for probability, severity, and timeline. Non-obvious risks identified. Interconnections between risks noted. |
| 5 | Exceptional risk analysis. Comprehensive risk register with probability, severity, speed of onset, and detection difficulty. Second-order effects and cascading risks identified. Risk interactions mapped. Reader has a complete picture of the legal risk landscape. |

---

## 4. Precedent & Authority Citation

What it measures: References to case law, regulatory guidance, enforcement actions, and authoritative legal sources.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No legal authority cited. Claims about legal standards are unsupported assertions. |
| 2 | Occasional references to laws or regulations but no case law or enforcement actions. Citations are vague ("courts have held...") without specifics. |
| 3 | Some specific legal authority cited. References to key statutes and regulations. Limited case law citations. May reference enforcement trends generally. |
| 4 | Strong authority citation. Specific statutes, regulations, and relevant case law cited. Enforcement actions referenced with outcomes. Regulatory guidance documents identified. |
| 5 | Rigorous legal authority. Specific case citations with holdings relevant to the analysis. Enforcement action precedents with penalty amounts. Regulatory guidance documents and agency interpretive positions cited. Distinguishes between binding authority and persuasive authority. |

---

## 5. Enforceability Assessment

What it measures: Evaluates practical enforceability of legal positions, remedies available, and real-world litigation outcomes.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No enforceability analysis. Legal positions stated as absolute without considering practical enforceability or available remedies. |
| 2 | Mentions enforceability in passing. Some awareness that legal rights and practical enforcement differ but no meaningful analysis. |
| 3 | Reasonable enforceability assessment. Identifies that some legal positions are stronger than others. Notes where enforcement is likely vs. unlikely. |
| 4 | Strong enforceability analysis. Assesses practical enforceability of key legal positions. Identifies available remedies and their effectiveness. Notes where enforcement varies by jurisdiction or context. |
| 5 | Expert enforceability assessment. Evaluates enforceability considering practical realities (litigation cost, burden of proof, jury attitudes, regulatory priorities). Identifies which remedies are actually effective. Assesses litigation risk vs. regulatory enforcement risk. Provides enforcement probability estimates where possible. |

---

## 6. Actionability

What it measures: Specific next steps, decision criteria, timeline recommendations, and clear identification of when professional counsel is needed.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | No actionable recommendations. Analysis is purely descriptive with no guidance on what to do next. |
| 2 | Vague recommendations ("consult a lawyer", "ensure compliance"). No specific next steps, timelines, or decision criteria. |
| 3 | Some concrete recommendations but lacking specifics. May have next steps OR timelines but not both. Does not clearly identify when counsel is needed vs. when executive action is appropriate. |
| 4 | Clear, actionable recommendations. Specific next steps with priority ordering. Timelines for compliance actions. Decision criteria for key choices. Clearly identifies which matters require professional counsel and which can be handled operationally. |
| 5 | Highly actionable legal playbook. Specific recommendations with timelines, cost ranges, responsible parties, and success criteria. Decision trees for key legal choices. Triage between "handle internally," "engage counsel," and "escalate immediately." Reader can begin implementation from the recommendations alone. |
